Route — I-90 W across South Dakota
The landscape transforms dramatically west of Sioux Falls. For the first two hours it's rolling green prairie — then around Mitchell and Chamberlain the terrain opens up into something vast and flat in a way that feels almost oceanic. The Missouri River crossing at Chamberlain is a genuine landscape event — the hills suddenly drop away and the river is wide and silver below. West of Chamberlain the sky becomes the dominant element. By the time you reach the Badlands exit the land is already half-broken and austere.

Badlands Loop Road — Hwy 240, 39 miles
The Loop Road is itself the attraction — 30 named overlooks, each showing a different face of the formations. The eastern end is the most dramatic; the western Sage Creek section is the most remote and wildlife-rich. Drive it at least twice: once in the morning when formations are bright white and orange, once in the late afternoon when they turn red and purple. The light changes the park entirely.

Route — Highway 85 North, the Dances with Wolves road
This is the most cinematic drive of the trip. Highway 85 north of Wall cuts through open shortgrass prairie with almost no traffic — exactly the landscape you'll recognize from the film. The road is ruler-straight for long stretches, then dips through creek valleys with cottonwoods, then opens back onto grass and sky. As you cross into North Dakota the terrain becomes more rolling and the potholes (small glacial lakes) multiply. The last hour into Medora, the badlands topography reappears as the Little Missouri River valley cuts through. Fill up in Wall before leaving — stations are sparse until Bowman.

South Unit Scenic Loop — 36 miles
The South Unit loop is the park's core drive and it's far better experienced slowly than efficiently. The road winds through dramatically eroded badlands, drops into the Little Missouri River valley, climbs back onto ridge overlooks, and passes through open prairie where bison roam completely freely. Unlike Badlands NP, the landscape here is inhabited by animals at every turn. Wind Canyon is the single most dramatic viewpoint — a short walk to a cliff edge above the river. Buck Hill is the panoramic apex. Drive at 10–15 mph and stop constantly.
06:30
Dawn wildlife drive — South Unit ↗
Bison and wild horses most active at first light, often blocking the road completely. Drive slowly from the first mile.
The horses here are genuinely feral. Seeing a herd on the badlands ridge in morning mist is one of those travel memories you keep for years.

Route — Hwy 85 South then Hwy 385 into the Black Hills
The return south on Hwy 85 has different light than the outbound drive — worth noticing. The moment you enter the Black Hills south of Belle Fourche is one of the great highway transitions: the flat Plains suddenly give way to dense ponderosa pine forest and granite outcrops, the road starts climbing and curving, and the temperature drops a few degrees. Highway 385 south through the Hills to Hill City is a beautiful mountain road — narrow, forested, with occasional granite formations visible through the trees.

Scenic drives inside Custer — three classics
Wildlife Loop Road (18 miles, open grassland) — the bison and wildlife drive. Needles Highway (14 miles, narrow mountain road) — granite spires and tunnels barely wide enough for a car. Iron Mountain Road (17 miles, back to Hill City) — pigtail bridges that spiral up the hillside, and three tunnels that perfectly frame Mount Rushmore in the distance. All three are slow drives by design — 10–15 mph in sections. Together they represent some of the most creative road engineering in the US.
06:30
Wildlife Loop Road at dawn ↗
From Hill City: Hwy 385 south ~10 min to Hwy 16A, east entrance. Ask at gate where wildlife was spotted. 18 miles — budget 2h30–3 hrs, not 45 min. Bison can stop traffic 10–20 min. Burros walk up to open windows.
1,300+ free-roaming bison. Pronghorn, bighorn sheep, prairie dogs throughout. Do not rush this.

OvernightHill City, SD
Route — Hwy 385 N, then Hwy 244 to Rushmore, then Hwy 385 N to Deadwood
The drive from Hill City to Crazy Horse and Rushmore passes through dense ponderosa pine forest with granite outcrops. The approach to Rushmore via Iron Mountain Road (Hwy 16A, worth taking if you didn't yesterday) has tunnels that deliberately frame the four presidents — the NPS designed it that way. The drive from Rushmore to Deadwood through Lead and Central City goes through old mining territory — the terrain becomes more dramatic and the towns more atmospheric as you climb north through the Hills.
09:00
Crazy Horse Memorial ↗
Opens 9am. Start with the bus to the base ($5 extra, ~10 min wait). The face is 87 feet high. 3 museums and orientation film. Not federally funded — built by private donations since 1948, still unfinished after 75 years. That story is more moving than the carving itself.
$15 + $5 bus~2 hrs
11:00
Mount Rushmore ↗
No entry fee — $10 parking only. ATB pass does NOT cover parking. Avenue of Flags, Grand View Terrace, Presidential Trail (0.6 miles). Visitor center film: 20 min, worth it.
$10 parking only~1h30
Optional — return for evening lighting ceremony
Rushmore is illuminated nightly until ~11pm in summer. The lighting ceremony with film and flag ceremony is a completely different atmosphere from daytime.
Optional return
13:00
Deadwood — lunch ↗
Local restaurant — Deadwood lunch
Deadwood Social Club ↗
Upstairs from Saloon No. 10. Italian-accented American cuisine in a polished wood-panelled room that feels like an elegant brothel (historically appropriate). Wild game, prime steaks, fried buffalo sausage ravioli. One of the best restaurants in the entire Black Hills region. Alternatively, Sugar Shack on Hwy 385 for the best burger in the Black Hills, according to locals.
~1 hr lunch
14:30
Deadwood attractions — choose your mix:
Adams Museum ↗
Free. Wild Bill Hickok's revolver, gold rush artifacts, a two-headed calf. Deadwood's oldest history museum. ~45 min.
Mount Moriah Cemetery ↗
~$1. Wild Bill and Calamity Jane buried here. Panoramic views over Deadwood. 5 min walk uphill from Main Street. ~40 min.
Saloon No. 10 + Main Street ↗
Wild Bill was shot here in 1876. Shootout reenactments daily at set times. 150+ whiskeys on the bar. The street is the genuine 1876 layout.
Days of '76 Museum
Exceptional collection of 19th-century stagecoaches, wagons, and rodeo history. One of the best western history museums in the region. ~1 hr.
17:30
Tatanka: Story of the Bison ↗
2 min drive from Deadwood. Outside: 14 life-size bronze bison stampeding, pursued by Lakota riders. Inside: film on bison near-extinction and Lakota culture.
Given your Dances with Wolves connection, this may be the most resonant single stop on the entire trip.
~$10~1 hr

Route options — I-90 W then Hwy 14 / and optional Spearfish Canyon
The direct route to Devil's Tower takes I-90 west to Spearfish, then Hwy 14 west into Wyoming — pleasant but unremarkable. The scenic option detours through Spearfish Canyon (Hwy 14A south off I-90) before rejoining west — a 20-mile limestone canyon with waterfalls and old-growth forest, genuinely beautiful, adds ~45 min. The drive back east on I-90 across South Dakota in late afternoon is a completely different experience from the morning drive outbound — the light comes from behind now, and the Missouri River crossing at Chamberlain looks different in evening gold.
