Porter Sculpture Park, Montrose — I-90 Exit 374 ↗ Maps
60+ massive scrap-metal sculptures on 10 prairie acres by self-taught artist Wayne Porter. The centerpiece is a 60-foot bull head — same scale as the Rushmore heads. Surreal in the middle of nowhere. Opens May 15.

Coal Vein Trail ↗ Maps
A coal seam burned underground for decades, baking surrounding rock into vivid red and orange scoria. Completely unlike anything in SD. 1.6 km loop, easy.
~1h30

Easy 1.5 km loop with great badlands overview. Excellent for photography.

16 km round trip to petrified wood formations. Long but very remote and rarely visited. Takes most of the afternoon.
North Unit — full detour
The less-visited North Unit is 1.5 hrs from Medora and has dramatic canyon views the South Unit lacks. Would require an early start and sacrificing the Coal Vein afternoon — but worth it if you want to see both units.

Animals move again as heat drops. Horses and bison most likely near the river flats. Completely different light than morning.

Day 8 · Saturday May 30
Crazy Horse + Rushmore + Deadwood
Crazy Horse17 min from Hill City
Rushmore20 min from Crazy Horse
Deadwood35 min from Rushmore
OvernightHill City, SD
09:00
Crazy Horse Memorial ↗ Maps
Opens 9am. Start with the bus to the base ($5 extra, departs when full — ~10 min wait). The face alone is 87 feet high. 3 museums and orientation film. Not federally funded — built by private donations since 1948, still unfinished after 75 years. That story is more moving than the carving itself.
$15 + $5 bus~2 hrs
11:00
Mount Rushmore ↗ Maps
No entry fee — $10 parking only. ATB pass does NOT cover parking. Avenue of Flags walk, Grand View Terrace, Presidential Trail (0.6 miles, takes you closer). Visitor center film: 20 min, worth it.
$10 parking only~1h30
Optional — evening lighting ceremony
Mount Rushmore is illuminated nightly in summer until ~11pm. If you want to return for the lighting ceremony later, it's worth it — completely different atmosphere. The program includes a film and flag ceremony.
Optional return visit
13:00
Deadwood — lunch ↗ Maps
35 min north on Hwy 385. Park once, walk everywhere. The 1876 Gold Rush street layout is genuine, not a recreation.
~1 hr lunch
14:30
Deadwood attractions — choose your mix:
Adams Museum ↗ Maps
Free. Wild Bill Hickok's revolver, gold rush artifacts, a two-headed calf. Deadwood's oldest history museum, genuinely good. ~45 min.
Mount Moriah Cemetery ↗ Maps
~$1. Wild Bill Hickok and Calamity Jane buried here. 5 min walk uphill from Main Street. Panoramic views over Deadwood and the Hills. ~40 min.
Saloon No. 10 + Main Street ↗ Maps
Wild Bill was shot here playing poker in 1876. Shootout reenactments daily at set times. The street is the genuine 1876 layout.
Days of '76 Museum
Exceptional collection of 19th-century stagecoaches, wagons, and rodeo history. One of the best western history museums in the region. ~1 hr.
17:30
Tatanka: Story of the Bison ↗ Maps
2 min drive from Deadwood. Outside: 14 life-size bronze bison stampeding, pursued by three Lakota riders — extraordinary scale. Inside: film and exhibits on bison near-extinction and Lakota culture.
Given your Dances with Wolves connection, this may be the most resonant single stop on the entire trip.
~$10~1 hr
19:30
Dinner in Deadwood or drive back to Hill City (~35 min)

Devil's Tower National Monument, WY ↗ Maps
Tower Trail: 2.5 km loop around the base (~1h30). The scale only registers when you're standing directly under it. Climbers sometimes visible high on the columns. America's first National Monument — declared by Theodore Roosevelt in 1906.
Photos genuinely do not prepare you for the scale. It is much larger than it appears on approach.
ATB pass covers it~2 hrs
Red Beds Trail
4.8 km loop through the surrounding landscape — views of the tower from a distance with Belle Fourche River below. More solitary than the Tower Trail. ~1.5 hrs.
Climbers watching
Devil's Tower is one of the most famous climbing destinations in the US. In late May climbers are often visible. The column cracks are the standard routes — ask rangers where to see them best.
